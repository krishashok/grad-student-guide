What makes a balanced meal?

A substantial protein, some vegetables or fruit, enough rice, bread or potatoes to fuel you, and flavour you will want again tomorrow.

The plate shows proportions, not compartments. Rajma chawal is one dish and still counts. Legumes are protein and carbohydrate at once. The fourth row is breakfast. Plain yoghurt can carry far less protein than Greek yoghurt or skyr, so compare the two labels. A thin spoonful of sambar is not the protein serving a bowl of thick dal is.

Which shortcuts are worth buying?

Frozen and canned vegetables count. Freezing stops microbes multiplying while the food stays frozen, and protein and fibre largely remain. The test below is for fully packaged food.

Chapter 3 · 04:26

How to spend less on food

Compare prices per kilogram or per 100 g, count what you will eat before it spoils, and pay a little for convenience when it stops you ordering in.

Enter your own shelf prices
Waste-adjusted cost

Cost per portion you eat

Price divided by the portions that reach your plate. A half-price bag that half rots costs the same per eaten portion as a full-price bag you finish.

Protein value

Price per 20 g of protein

Package price ÷ grams of protein in the package × 20. Use drained weight for cans. This measures protein value only. Twenty grams is a comparison unit, not a meal target.

Chapter 4 · 05:30

How long can leftovers wait?

Cook extra, cool it fast in shallow containers, and freeze what you will not eat in the next day or two.

Leftover clock

When to refrigerate, when to eat

Fridge 4°C / 40°F or colder Freezer −18°C / 0°F or colder Reheat to 74°C / 165°F throughout, stir, check the centre Thaw in the fridge, or microwave and heat immediately

The 1–2 day fridge routine is deliberately conservative for an international audience. USDA allows 3–4 days for properly handled leftovers; UK guidance says 48 hours. The rice rule follows the UK Food Standards Agency: cool within an hour, 24 hours in the fridge at most, reheat once. Freezing and reheating do not make badly stored food safe.

Chapter 6 · 08:59

What to eat when you are exhausted

Six meals that need a microwave at most. Each one has a protein, and each one gets its flavour from a condiment you already own.

Breakfast Soak 10 min

Dahi poha

  • Thick curd or Greek yoghurt
  • Poha
  • Roasted peanuts
  • Green chilli, salt, lime

Stir the poha into the curd and leave it until soft. For a sweet version, use banana and jaggery instead of chilli and salt. Oats work if the Indian shop is far.

Lunch Microwave 2 min

Curd rice from a pouch

  • Microwave rice
  • Curd
  • Roasted chana
  • Pickle

Heat the rice as the packet says, let it cool a little, stir in the curd and salt. Pickle supplies the flavour. Roasted chana or a handful of pomegranate supplies what the rice lacks.

Any meal No heat

Chana chaat

  • Canned chickpeas, rinsed
  • Onion, tomato, cucumber
  • Chaat masala
  • Green or tamarind chutney

Half a can gives about 7 g of protein and 6 g of fibre. Rinsing removes much of the sodium. Lime and chutney do the rest.

Dinner No heat

Hummus plate, achar edition

  • Wholegrain bread or frozen chapati
  • Hummus
  • Ready-to-eat tofu or paneer
  • Pickle oil or green chutney

Hummus is chickpeas, so a spoon of pickle oil or green chutney stirred in works. Cucumber and onion with chaat masala on the side. Follow the packet’s storage instructions for the tofu.

Dinner No heat

Tinned fish on toast

  • Tinned sardines or mackerel
  • Wholegrain toast
  • Raw onion
  • Green chilli, lime, coriander

Usually the cheapest fish in the supermarket, and small fish score high on the Masala Body chart. Mash with onion, chilli and lime and it is close to a sardine masala without a stove.

Breakfast Microwave 30 s

Peanut butter chapati roll

  • Frozen chapati
  • Peanut butter
  • Banana
  • Cinnamon or jaggery

Warm the chapati, spread, roll. Peanut butter brings the protein and fat, the banana the carbohydrate. Eat it on the way to the 8 am class.

If money is tight, check what food support your university offers and whether you are eligible. Pizza with friends is still allowed. What you eat regularly matters far more than any one meal.

Four things every expert agrees on

Learning to cook, and finding a stress-free way to do it regularly, is what makes the fourth one possible for the rest of your life.

  1. Enough sleep
  2. Enough exercise
  3. Not much stress
  4. Balanced nutrition. Not too many calories, and enough protein and fibre.
