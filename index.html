What makes a balanced meal?

A substantial protein, some vegetables or fruit, enough starch to fuel you, and something that makes you want to eat it again tomorrow.

Meal builder

One from each column. That’s dinner.

Rough composition, not compartments. A rajma chawal does not need to be served in three piles. Legumes count as protein and carbohydrate at once. The fourth row is breakfast. Plain yoghurt can carry much less protein than Greek yoghurt or skyr, so compare the labels.

Reading a label

Compare like with like, in this order

Same product type, same quantity. Two yoghurts, two breads. Look at protein and fibre first, then added sugar, sodium and saturated fat. Then check the calories in the portion you will actually eat, not the one printed on the pack.

The 5% / 20% rule is for US labels. Elsewhere, use the per-100 g column to compare products. Label rules are not identical everywhere.

Protein per calorie

Twenty grams in 200 calories beats ten

If you are buying something specifically for protein, compare how much protein you get for the calories and the price. A food with 20 g of protein in 200 kcal gives you twice as much as one with 10 g in the same calories.

13.3 gprotein per 200 kcal

Nuts are nutritious, with good fats, fibre and minerals, but they are not your cheapest main protein. A watery spoonful of sambar is not the same as a thick bowl of dal or a sundal.

Which shortcuts are worth buying?

When you pick up a packet, ask how much of the composition of your meal has already been decided for you. Then check what it is made of.

The five finger test

Take 200 calories of it. Count the fingers.

For fully packaged food, type in the numbers from the label for one serving. The test scales them to 200 kcal and awards one finger for each line the food passes.

0 / 5Instant noodles

Under 2 is deadline, flu or heartbreak food. 2 or 3 is workable if you add an egg, tofu or frozen veg and use less of a salty seasoning. 4 or 5 is a meal. Preset values are illustrative labels, not any specific brand. Rinsing canned beans drops the sodium a lot.

Frozen and canned

Freezing is not sterilising

Freezing stops microbes multiplying while the food stays frozen and slows the reactions that spoil it. Protein and fibre largely remain, and many vitamins hold up well.

Canning uses heat and a sealed container, so some vitamins are lost, but the beans remain nutritious.

If you regularly throw away fresh spinach because you never cooked it in time, frozen spinach is not a compromise. It is the better product for you.

How to spend less on food

Compare unit prices, count what you will actually eat before it spoils, and pay a little for convenience when it saves you from ordering in.

All figures below are your own inputs, not local prices
Shelf price

The big packet is not automatically cheaper

Compare per kilogram, litre or 100 g. Supermarket own brands are worth trying for staples.

Waste-adjusted cost

Half-price salad you throw away

A giant bag at half price saves nothing if most of it goes in the bin. Effective cost is price divided by the portions you actually eat.

Protein value

Price per 20 g of protein

Package price ÷ total grams of protein in the package × 20. Use drained weight for cans. This measures protein value only, not overall quality. Twenty grams is a comparison unit, not a meal target.

Storage before cooking

Where does it live?

Eat the delicate fresh vegetables first. Carrots and cabbage wait patiently. Restaurant dal will likely have about three times the calories of the one you make at home, so paying a little for canned beans that stop you ordering in can reduce what you spend overall.

First shopping basket

Six lines, not a mandate

    Choose what fits local prices and your cooking facilities. You do not need imported Indian versions of everything: local dal and vegetables take Indian spices perfectly well. Check your destination’s current food-import and declaration rules before packing anything edible.

    How to cook less often

    Cook once, eat three times. Use the freezer. And do the exact opposite of the uncle who won’t eat food from the fridge.

    Leftover clock

    Cooked at what time? Here is your deadline.

    Fridge 4°C / 40°F or colder Freezer −18°C / 0°F or colder Reheat to 74°C / 165°F throughout, stir, check the centre Thaw in the fridge, or microwave and heat immediately

    Cool food in shallow containers. Never leave a big pot cooling overnight. The 1–2 day fridge routine is deliberately conservative for an international audience: USDA allows 3–4 days for properly handled leftovers and the UK commonly says 48 hours. Rice follows the UK Food Standards Agency: cool within an hour, fridge for 24 hours at most, reheat only once. Freezing and reheating will not make badly stored food safe again. Keep raw meat away from ready-to-eat food. For dried kidney beans, soak and boil properly; canned kidney beans are already cooked and simpler.

    How to make this a routine

    Knowing all this does not stop you opening the delivery app. You need a routine that survives being tired.

    The doorway

    Same cue, different routine

    Repeat an action in a familiar situation and the situation starts triggering the action with less thought, the way your fingers type your phone passcode. The cue is the same in both rows. Only the next two steps change.

    Old loop
    Bag down→Sofa→Delivery app
    New loop
    Bag down→Fridge→Microwave
    When Iwalk in the doorI start heating a meal I chose in advance, before I sit down.
    When Ifinish dinnerI pack tomorrow’s lunch while the pan is still out.
    When Itake out the last frozen mealI add its ingredients to the shopping list.

    It helps if the result is enjoyable straight away: good food, little washing up, money saved. Your health at fifty is a distant abstraction when you are twenty-two and hungry. Start with one routine, perhaps breakfast, and give it time. There is no universal 21-day deadline.
